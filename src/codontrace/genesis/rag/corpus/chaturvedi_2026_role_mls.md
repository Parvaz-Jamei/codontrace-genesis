## Digest

Chaturvedi, El-Gazzar, and van Gerven (arXiv 2604.00810, 2026) show that multilevel selection can maintain role differentiation in a common-pool ecology and avoid collapse into a single resource channel (tragedy of the commons). Agents share a controller substrate and mutation operator shaped by group-level selection while individuals still compete. Two coupled channels — positive-sum intake (grazing) and zero-sum redistribution (exchange) — stay occupied at colony level. Roles are dynamic: individuals switch over a lifetime while the group keeps a stable mix. Ablations suggest inherited behavioral basis carries most performance; learned variation adds a smaller systematic gain. This is a 2026 computational MLS result CodonTrace Genesis does *not* replicate. Assigned round-robin roles are the opposite of dynamic coupled-channel differentiation. Phase H group-vs-individual campaigns with effect sizes are the measurement on-ramp, not the paper. Collective intelligence remains blocked.

## Key claims

- MLS can sustain differentiated use of coupled resource channels under individual competition.
- Roles need not be castes; dynamic switching with stable group mix is a result, not a tag.
- Ablate group-level selection before claiming the mechanism.

## Digest

Miikkulainen and Forrest evaluate how evolutionary computation (EC) diverges from biological evolution. Three structural barriers dominate: (1) typical EC uses small populations and strong selection, whereas biology retains diversity under weaker selection, drift, and neutrality; (2) typical EC uses a nearly direct genotype-to-phenotype map, whereas biology uses developmental and regulatory maps with many-to-one encodings and cryptic variation; (3) biology discovers major organizational transitions (new levels of individuality), whereas EC almost always stays at one organizational level. The paper sits with the longer computational-evolution agenda (Banzhaf et al., Nature Reviews Genetics 2006). Crossing these barriers is not a hyperparameter tweak. Small-N smokes that maximize a fitness function faster are evidence of selection pressure, not of biological-scale evolution. Direct instruction genomes that decode into actions remain a direct G-P map even if the instruction set is rich. Group containers that a researcher tags are not a major transition until a new Darwinian individual appears.

## Key claims

- Small populations plus strong selection strip the diversity biology uses for open exploration.
- Direct genotype-phenotype maps skip development, neutrality, and many-to-one encodings.
- Absence of major transitions in individuality is a first-class EC failure mode, not a missing metric.
- Computational evolution as an agenda is not satisfied by better GA hyperparameters.

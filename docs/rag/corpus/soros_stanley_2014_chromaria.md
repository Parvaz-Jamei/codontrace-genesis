## Digest

Soros and Stanley used Chromaria to hypothesize four necessary conditions for open-ended evolution: (1) a nontrivial minimal criterion (MC) for reproduction that the seed itself must meet; (2) evolution of new individuals should create novel opportunities to satisfy the MC; (3) individuals decide how and where they interact with the world; (4) potential phenotypic size/complexity is in principle unbounded. Follow-up empirical work tests that removing a condition causes stagnation. An MC analog in a library (AliveGate, ATP birth gates) is not Chromaria condition 1 unless the seed must earn birth and the criterion is nontrivial. Condition 2 (self-generated opportunity landscapes) is the hardest and is not shown by researcher-scheduled resource fluctuations. Condition 3 is not satisfied by a tiny fixed action set on a 2D grid. Condition 4 is false of fixed codon-width instruction genomes. Passing a birth gate is not OEE.

## Key claims

- OEE requires a nontrivial MC that the seed meets, not a copy instruction.
- New individuals must create new ways to satisfy the MC (self-generated opportunities).
- Agent-directed interaction and unbounded phenotypic potential are necessary, not optional polish.
- Controlled ablation of each condition is the Chromaria-grade test.

## What CodonTrace Genesis has

AliveGate + runtime-ATP gates (MC analog); spatial eat/wait/copy; Phase C fluctuating environments; variable-genome tokens as a bounded complexity hook; Phase D MODES/Bedau after persistence.

## What CodonTrace Genesis lacks

Chromaria-style knock-out tests of all four conditions; self-generated opportunity landscapes; unbounded phenotypic potential; agent-directed interaction at Chromaria depth.
